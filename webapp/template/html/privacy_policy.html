PRIVACY POLICY

HappYness Project Community Management System

Last updated: January 2026

1. About this policy

HappYness Project (“we”, “us”) is a community organization in the Philippines focused on education, livelihood support, and community care. This policy explains what personal information our web-based community management system collects, why we collect it, how we protect it, and what choices you have. We follow the Data Privacy Act of 2012 (Republic Act No. 10173) and its Implementing Rules and Regulations.

It applies to beneficiaries, volunteers, donors, and anyone who visits or uses our website and system.

2. Information we collect

Beneficiaries: Full name, age or birthdate, address, contact number, email, household and family details, and information about your needs (for example education, livelihood, or assistance requested). We may also collect sensitive personal information, such as health or income details, only when it is needed to assess and deliver assistance.

Volunteers: Full name, contact details, address, skills, availability, and activity records.

Donors: Name, contact details, donation amount, and payment reference. We do not store full card or bank credentials.

Website visitors: Messages sent through our contact details, and basic technical data such as browser type and pages visited.

We collect only what is needed for the purposes below.

3. How we use your information

  • To process and evaluate beneficiary and volunteer applications.
  • To plan, run, and record programs, events, and distributions.
  • To contact you about your application, activities, or donation.
  • To issue acknowledgments for donations and keep accurate records.
  • To prepare reports that do not identify individuals (for example, number of families helped).
  • To keep the system secure and meet legal obligations.

We do not sell your personal information or use it for unrelated marketing.

4. Consent and legal basis

We process your information based on your consent, which you give when you submit a form or application, and where necessary to carry out our programs or comply with the law. You may withdraw consent at any time by contacting us (see section 10). Withdrawing may mean we can no longer process your application or provide certain assistance.

5. Who can access your information

Access is limited to authorized HappYness Project officers and volunteers who need it for their role. We may share information with:

  • Partner organizations, schools, or government agencies, only when needed to deliver the assistance you applied for and with your knowledge.
  • Service providers that host or support our system, under agreements requiring them to protect your data.
  • Authorities, when required by law or a lawful order.

Photos or stories from our activities are published only with consent, and with a parent or guardian’s consent for minors.

6. Storage, security, and retention

We use reasonable organizational, physical, and technical safeguards, including account-based access controls and limiting access by role. No system is completely secure, but we work to prevent unauthorized access, loss, or misuse.

We keep personal information only as long as needed for the purposes above or as required by law: [for example, beneficiary and volunteer records for X years after your last activity; donation records for X years]. After that, we securely delete or anonymize it.

If a data breach that may put you at risk occurs, we will notify you and the National Privacy Commission as required by law.

7. Your rights

Under the Data Privacy Act, you have the right to:

  • Be informed about how your data is processed.
  • Access the personal information we hold about you.
  • Correct inaccurate or outdated information.
  • Object to processing or withdraw your consent.
  • Request that your data be blocked, removed, or destroyed, where applicable.
  • Request a copy of your data in a usable format (data portability).
  • Be indemnified for damages caused by inaccurate, unlawfully obtained, or unauthorized use of your data.
  • File a complaint with the National Privacy Commission.

8. Children

Where a beneficiary is under 18, we collect information through a parent or legal guardian, who must give consent on the child’s behalf. We handle children’s information with extra care and limit who can see it.
